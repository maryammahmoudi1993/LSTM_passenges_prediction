## Results (from the executed notebook; test months 1959 to 1960; MAE in thousands of passengers; neural models: mean of 5 seeds and the smallest to the largest value)

| Model | Task A: one month ahead | Task B: 24 months ahead |
|---|---|---|
| naive / seasonal naive | 44.2 / 47.6 | 115.2 / 71.2 |
| SARIMA / Holt-Winters / Prophet | 12.5 / 12.2 / 17.6 | 42.0 / 29.0 / 25.5 |
| LSTM on levels | 69.8 (55.6 to 86.0) | 70.2 (65.4 to 76.8) |
| Transformer on levels | 47.0 (40.1 to 50.4) | 85.9 (67.9 to 102.3) |
| **LSTM on year-over-year changes** | **11.7 (11.3 to 12.4)** | **16.0 (12.3 to 24.2)** |
| Transformer on year-over-year changes | 15.9 (14.8 to 17.4) | 30.0 (21.4 to 45.1) |

- The representation mattered more than the model type: on levels the networks fail; on year-over-year changes the LSTM is the best model, tied with Holt-Winters in task A and clearly ahead in task B (with a large spread over seeds).
- The year-over-year representation contains the seasonal-naive idea by construction; part of the success is structure.
- The small Transformer is on par with the classical models at best; its attention is almost uniform (about 1/12 for each month), so it explains nothing here.
- Running the notebook twice gave identical printed numbers (timings aside).

## Doubts / open points

- Only about 100 training windows: a tiny data set for deep learning; results for the Transformer in particular may differ with more data.
- One series and 24 test months: only large gaps are safe conclusions; the seed-to-seed spread of the neural models is large in task B.
- The classical models in notebook 02 use the settings chosen in notebook 01 on its validation months; the neural sizes and representation were chosen on the validation months in notebook 02.
- Data licence is still unverified (see `data/README.md`).
